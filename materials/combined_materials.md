# B组 SUE 理论培训

“有策略地使用证据法”Strategic Use of Evidence (SUE)是一个能帮助审讯者更好区分受审者是否在说谎的审讯技巧。SUE技巧通过诱发受审者陈述之间的不一致（即受审者后续陈述与前期陈述不一致）以及陈述与证据之间的不一致（即受审者的陈述与已有证据不一致)来达成上述目标。以上两种不一致均经研究证实为说谎的可靠标志。审讯者需在审讯中尽可能隐瞒已有证据，并使用漏斗型提问框架来诱发上述两种不一致。尽管在审讯中说谎者和诚实者都试图说服审讯者他们是清白的，SUE技巧的一个基本前提是两者会使用不同策略来达成以上目的。研究表明，说谎者如有机会，会回避重要信息，而诚实者在审讯中会乐于提供信息且实话实说。例如，在回答一个开放性问题（“你上周四做了什么？”）的时候，说谎者会避免说出他曾经接近过犯罪现场（定罪细节），而一个诚实者不会回避说出他在犯罪现场（是定罪细节，但不会定罪于无辜者，所以他们没有理由避免揭露这个信息）。这意味着说谎者与诚实者之间的区别在于说谎者会有所回避。

如果说谎者无法回避定罪信息，他们则会否认它。例如，在回答一个具体问题“你当时是否去过Johnson的家里（犯罪现场）？”时，说谎者会否认他去过那里，而诚实者不会否认。这类问题会使说谎者否认事实，而这清晰地体现了陈述与证据之间的不一致（即审讯者有证据证明说谎者的陈述是错误的），从而可以判断受审者在说谎。具体问题也可以用来尽可能多地获得对证据的其他解释（包括可以使受审者排除嫌疑的解释）。

综上所述，说谎者在审讯中会有所回避及否认，而诚实者会乐于提供信息并实话实说。使用开放性问题可以反映出两者回答中省略的不同，而使用具体问题可以使说谎者否认事实或改变原来的口供。

提问策略：

为了突出说谎者和诚实者的回答策略的不同，SUE技巧主要有以下原则：

1）审讯者必须隐瞒已掌握的相关信息（证据）。当说谎者不清楚审讯者知道多少信息时，如果他们的陈述与已掌握的证据不一致，他们的回避和否认将会非常明显。例如，如果审讯者已经掌握了嫌疑人曾出现在犯罪现场附近的证据，审讯者不应透露这个信息。如果嫌疑人在回答一个问题时否认曾出现在犯罪现场附近，那么嫌疑人的陈述与证据之间已经产生了不一致。诚实者则不会省略他们曾出现在犯罪现场附近的信息，因为他们认为这个信息不会使他们负罪。

2）使用漏斗型的提问结构。使用漏斗型的提问结构可以有效使陈述与证据之间的不一致及陈述之间的不一致的次数最大化。这意味着审讯者在开始时应让嫌疑人自由陈述他的活动，然后逐渐提出更加具体的与证据相关的问题。这样的提问结构使嫌疑人有机会与证据相矛盾，且更为重要的是，这为无辜者提供了一个解释证据的机会。当越来越多的证据被审讯者揭露（“我们知道你曾在某地”），说谎者将要么否认这个信息，要么改变口供。诚实者则由始至终乐于提供信息并实话实说，且不会改变口供，也不会作出与证据不一致的陈述。反之，如果审讯者在审讯开始时便出示证据，说谎者则有机会将证据包含在他们的口供中。因而，使用漏斗型的提问结构可以最大化说谎者需要改变口供或否认证据的次数。

在构建审讯时，使用“证据框架矩阵”Evidence Framing Matrix (EFM)可以帮助将漏斗型提问结构实际应用于审讯过程中。

“证据框架矩阵”Evidence Framing Matrix (EFM)：任何一种证据都能以不同形式被出示。例如，证明嫌疑人在现场的DNA证据可以直接被出示（“我们有DNA证据表明你曾在Turku的中央图书馆”）或以一种更笼统的方式被出示（“我们有信息表明你曾在Turku”）。证据框架矩阵阐释了可能的出示证据的方法。这个矩阵有2个维度：证据来源的强度（由弱至强）以及证据的具体程度（由低至高）。

使用EFM矩阵及SUE审讯技巧的典型审讯结构是：由宽泛笼统的问题开始（例如，你上周五[案发日]曾去过哪里、做了什么？），然后顺着矩阵的方向由“弱来源、低具体程度”至“强来源、低具体程度”，最终至“强来源、高具体程度”的方式出示证据。使用逐步揭露证据的技巧的目的是诱使有罪的嫌疑人面对出示的证据改变口供，从而使疑人的陈述之间产生不一致。

例如，如果审讯者已经掌握了一个毛发样本，它可以证明嫌疑人曾到过MaryDover谋杀案案发的同一房间，审讯者可以使用EFM来构建审讯如下：审讯开始时，审讯者应宽泛地询问嫌疑人在案发日的活动。如果嫌疑人开始告诉审讯者他一整天都在他姐妹的房子里，一个陈述与证据之间的不一致已经产生了。如果嫌疑人有对证据的解释能够排除其嫌疑的，此时解释应该已经出现了。接着，审讯者应表示他有信息（弱来源）表明嫌疑人当时在案发的相同地区（低具体程度）。在第一个问题中说谎的嫌疑人要么会否认这个信息，要么会改变口供来匹配这个新出现的证据，而这对于无辜者来说是另一个回忆当日活动的机会。

下一步，审讯者应以强来源、低具体程度的方式出示证据。审讯者可告诉嫌疑人他已经在那个区域获得了与嫌疑人匹配的毛发样本。如果嫌疑人否认了上一个问题中的证据，他的陈述则再次与证据不一致。此时，他可能会承认曾去过那个区域，并作出一些其他的解释。而这种情况下，嫌疑人陈述之间的不一致也产生了。

将矩阵应用于本案例的最后一步是以强来源、高具体程度的方式出示证据。审讯者可告诉嫌疑人：“我们在Mary的房间里发现了你的毛发样本。”一个说谎的嫌疑人会再次改变口供来匹配这个证据，而诚实者的陈述则全程保持一致。

《常规嫌疑人访谈与真伪鉴别指南》

在针对违法或犯罪嫌疑人的调查访谈中，访谈员的核心目标是查明真相并判断嫌疑人是否有罪。为了有效突破嫌疑人的心理防线并鉴别其陈述的真伪，常规访谈通常遵循以下基本原则和步骤：

一、 建立行为基线 (Establishing a Baseline) 在切入案件正题之前，访谈员必须先与嫌疑人进行简短的日常对话（例如询问其基本信息、工作或当天的天气）。这一步的目的不是为了拉近关系，而是为了观察嫌疑人在回答真实、无压力问题时的正常状态（即“基线”）。您需要重点记住他们此时的语速、眼神接触频率以及肢体动作，以便在后续高压提问中进行对比。

二、 直接提问与获取陈述 (Direct Questioning and Account Elicitation) 度过基线阶段后，访谈员应直接将话题转向调查事件。

明确主题： 清楚地告知嫌疑人目前正在调查的事件，并直接要求其说明在案发时间段的行踪。

施加认知压力： 有罪的嫌疑人在编造谎言时通常会消耗更多的脑力。您应当要求他们尽可能详细地描述整个过程。如果嫌疑人的回答过于简略，应不断追问细节（如：“你当时具体走的是哪条路？”“你到达那个房间后做的第一件事是什么？”）。

三、 识别欺骗信号 (Identifying Signals of Deception) 判断嫌疑人是否有罪的关键在于捕捉其偏离“基线”的异常表现。在面对核心问题时，试图隐瞒真相的嫌疑人往往会暴露出以下可疑信号：

言语特征： 频繁的停顿、结巴、清嗓子；回答问题前重复您的问题（以争取思考时间）；使用模糊的防御性词汇（如“我尽量回忆”、“据我所知”）。

非言语特征： 突然回避眼神交流或刻意死盯访谈员；出现坐立不安、摸鼻子、整理衣服等掩饰性动作；身体姿态从开放转向封闭（如双手抱胸）。

逻辑矛盾： 仔细倾听其故事中的逻辑漏洞。谎言往往难以做到前后绝对一致，多次盘问同一细节有助于暴露其陈述中的内在矛盾。

四、 做出判断 (Evaluating Guilt) 在访谈结束时，访谈员需要综合嫌疑人的陈述逻辑、回答问题时的态度（是坦诚配合还是充满敌意/回避），以及捕捉到的欺骗信号，对其“有罪”或“无罪”做出最终的专业判断。

Scenario A — Theft at a Sailing Club Open Day

Overview

Setting: A small sailing club holds its annual Open Day. Compact binoculars (Steiner 8×30, ~£200) are stolen from an unattended sailboat, the Sea Whisper at Berth 12, Pontoon C. The owner is out racing; the boat is unlocked.

Both suspects attended the Open Day. No registration, no financial trace. Both paid cash for a coffee at the marina café (no card record), walked along Pontoon C with other visitors, and appeared in the club’s social media post from the event. The guilty suspect also boarded the Sea Whisper and removed the binoculars — captured by no evidence piece.

Guilty: Attended as cover after identifying the owner on the racing programme. Paid cash for coffee, walked Pontoon C, boarded the Sea Whisper unseen, took the binoculars.

Innocent: Attended out of genuine interest in sailing. Paid cash for coffee, walked Pontoon C to view the boats, admired the Sea Whisper from the pontoon. Did not board any boat.

Parallel activity structure:

Evidence file (identical for both conditions):

E1 — Café worker’s recollection (anchor, predictable, uncertain): Worker recalls a customer matching the suspect’s description who paid cash around 13:00–13:15 and asked which pontoon had the older traditional boats. Both paid cash; both expected no financial trace. The worker’s memory is the unexpected piece.

E2 — Club member, Pontoon B (less predictable): A member eating lunch in his cockpit on the adjacent pontoon noticed the suspect walking Pontoon C toward the far end at a visitor’s pace. Applies identically to both conditions.

E3 — Club social media photo (less predictable): Open Day photo posted at 13:31 shows the suspect identifiable near Berths 10–12. Neither knew the photo was being taken. Both appear in it for different reasons.

EFM Analysis: Evidence 2 — Club Member Witness, Pontoon C

Less predictable. Both walked Pontoon C — the innocent following the café worker’s direction, the guilty heading for Berth 12. The witness’s observation is identical for both conditions: someone walking toward the far end, looking at boats. The innocent confirms the walk effortlessly; the guilty tends to deny Pontoon C because being placed there — combined with the café evidence — begins closing the inferential gap.

EFM Analysis: Evidence 3 — Club Social Media Photo, Near Berth 12

Less predictable: neither knew the photo was being taken. Both visible on Pontoon C near Berths 10–12 at 13:31. Visual, timestamped, hard to dispute. For the innocent this is expected — they walked the full pontoon and the Sea Whisper caught their attention. For the guilty, the photo directly contradicts any denial of Pontoon C. Presented last, it is the visual confirmation of what E1 and E2 have already established.

Scenario B — Arson at a Sports Club Pavilion

Overview

Setting: A community sports club pavilion is deliberately set alight in the early hours of Sunday morning. Fire investigators confirm accelerant (hydrocarbon, consistent with barbecue lighter fluid) was used. The fire started around 23:15. The pavilion is a total loss. Both suspects are club members.

Both suspects claimed to be at home that evening and posted to Instagram Stories. Both had a garden barbecue or bonfire earlier that same day. Both drove somewhere later that evening. The criminal act — driving to the pavilion, applying accelerant, and igniting it — is captured by no evidence piece. The fire destroys any trace of presence at the scene itself.

Guilty: Spent the early evening at home, photographed the living room at 19:22 as a deliberate alibi image, drove to the pavilion and posted the photo at 22:47 from the car to create the impression of being home. Set the fire around 23:15. The phone pinged the Kellaway Road mast at 23:09 from the pavilion car park.

Innocent: Spent the evening genuinely at home. Had a barbecue in the afternoon, wore the same jacket outside. At 19:22 took a casual photo of the living room in the afternoon light and thought nothing more of it. Posted it at 22:47 while scrolling their phone on the sofa. Around 23:00 drove to Kellaway Road to pick up a friend from the pub, passing through the mast’s coverage area.

Parallel activity structure:

Evidence file (identical for both conditions):

E1 — Instagram Stories post: EXIF timestamp inconsistency (anchor, uncertain): Both suspects posted a photo to Instagram Stories at 22:47 showing a domestic indoor scene, appearing to show them at home that evening. Investigators examining the image file found that its embedded EXIF metadata records the photo as captured at 19:22 — three hours and twenty-five minutes before it was posted. Both suspects photographed their living room at 19:22 and posted it at 22:47; neither expected the image file’s metadata to be examined. The inconsistency is real but its interpretation is genuinely contested: delayed posting of an earlier photo is extremely common and entirely innocent. The uncertainty is interpretive — casual habit vs. deliberate alibi construction — not identificatory.

E2 — Accelerant trace on jacket (less predictable): Both suspects wore the same jacket that day and evening. Chemical analysis of the jacket found traces of a hydrocarbon accelerant consistent with barbecue lighter fluid. Both suspects used lighter fluid earlier that day — the innocent for a garden barbecue, the guilty for the same barbecue plus, later, the pavilion. Neither anticipated clothing from that day being chemically tested. The uncertainty is about source: the same substance serves both purposes, and the trace is chemically indistinguishable between barbecue use and arson use. There is no way to determine from the trace alone which fire it came from, or whether it came from both.

E3 — Cell tower ping, Kellaway Road mast (less predictable): Mobile phone records show the suspect’s handset registered against the Kellaway Road mast at 23:29. This mast’s primary coverage footprint includes the sports ground and the surrounding 800-metre radius, which also encompasses Kellaway Road itself and adjacent residential streets. Both suspects’ phones registered this mast at approximately that time: the innocent because they drove to Kellaway Road to collect a friend from the pub; the guilty because they were at the pavilion car park 400 metres from the mast. The ping is certain as to the handset; it is uncertain as to precise location within the mast’s footprint. Neither suspected their phone records would be checked.
